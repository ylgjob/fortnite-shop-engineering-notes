How to Compare Fortnite Shop Prices Across Rotations

AI-assisted guide checked against SkinRotation’s offer data model. The prices and offers below are fictional.

If a Fortnite outfit appears at 1,500 V-Bucks one day and at 1,800 on the next, the outfit’s price may have changed. It may also be part of a different bundle. A tracker can distinguish the two by recording what each price actually buys.

Decide which offers are comparable

For each date, record the offer ID, the cosmetic IDs it includes, and its current price in V-Bucks. Compare prices only after checking that the two offers contain the same items. Keep the regular price as a separate field so that a comparison between days always uses the amount the shopper would have paid on each day.

  • Same offer and same contents. A standalone outfit at 1,500 on Monday and 1,200 on Tuesday is a 300 V-Buck decrease in the recorded offer price.
  • Same outfit in a different purchase. A standalone outfit at 1,500 on Monday and a bundle containing the outfit and a pickaxe at 1,800 on Tuesday are different offers. Report the switch to a bundle. There is no standalone Tuesday price to compare.
  • Missing Tuesday snapshot. Keep Monday’s price as the last observed price. Leave the change unverified until you can inspect Tuesday’s rotation.

Check the comparison in an archive

Open two dates in the SkinRotation Fortnite Item Shop archive. Match the included cosmetics before comparing prices, and note whether each listing is a solo offer or a bundle. A useful report records both dates, both offer names, and the reason the prices are comparable.
